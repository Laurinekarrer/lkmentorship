A practical method

Come back to what your body already knows.

Sophrology brings body, breath and mind back into conversation.

Created in 1960 by Colombian neuropsychiatrist Alfonso Caycedo, sophrology offers a structured way to observe your experience, ease accumulated pressure and develop inner resources you can use beyond the session.

Laurine Karrer is a certified sophrologist trained over three years with Sophrocap. Her approach is informed by clinical support, float sessions, bodywork and her own lived understanding of nervous-system regulation across change and different cultural environments.

For life in Amsterdam

When your mind keeps going, but your body asks you to slow down.

This work is particularly relevant for professionals, entrepreneurs, creatives and internationals navigating a demanding rhythm, cultural change or a personal transition.

You may arrive feeling stressed, mentally crowded, emotionally overloaded, disconnected from your body or unable to fully rest. Sophrology does not ask you to force a positive mindset. It gives you concrete ways to work with breath, sensation, movement and attention so that regulation becomes something you can practise—not simply understand.

Sessions are available in Amsterdam and online, in English or French.

Frequently asked questions.

What is sophrology?+

Sophrology is a structured mind-body practice combining conscious breathing, muscular relaxation, gentle movement, guided imagery and focused awareness.

Can I practise sophrology in English in Amsterdam?+

Yes. Laurine offers individual sophrology sessions in Amsterdam in English and French, as well as online sessions.

What happens during a sophrology session?+

A session begins with a conversation, followed by practices adapted to your needs. These may include breathing, dynamic relaxation, gentle movement and guided imagery, with time afterwards to notice and integrate your experience.

Is sophrology a replacement for medical care or psychotherapy?+

No. Sophrology is a complementary form of support and does not replace medical diagnosis, treatment or psychological care.
